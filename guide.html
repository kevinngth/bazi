The eight characters

BaZi means eight characters. Your birth moment is written as four pairs of Chinese characters, and those eight characters are the whole chart. Everything a reader says comes out of them.

Each pair is called a pillar (柱), and there are four: year, month, day and hour. Hence the other name, the Four Pillars of Destiny (四柱命理).

The top character of each pillar is a heavenly stem (天干). There are ten of them, and they are simply the five elements in a yang and a yin version:

Stems and branches turn together like two gears, ten teeth against twelve. They return to the same pairing every sixty steps, which is why the sexagenary cycle (六十甲子) is sixty long and why a sixtieth birthday is a milestone: your year pillar has come round to where it started.

Where each pillar comes from

Nothing here is arbitrary. Each pillar is derived by a fixed rule, and if you know the rules you can check any calculator's work, including ours.

The year pillar

Runs on the sixty-cycle, one step per year. The catch is when the year turns: not 1 January, and not Chinese New Year, but 立春 (Lichun, "start of spring"), which falls around 4 February. See the next section: this trips up more people than anything else in BaZi.

The month pillar

The branch is fixed to the season, always in the same order: 寅 for the month beginning at 立春, then 卯, 辰 and round. The stem is derived from the year stem by a rule called 五虎遁 ("hiding the five tigers"), which is really just a mnemonic table: a 甲 or 己 year always starts its first month with 丙, a 乙 or 庚 year with 戊, and so on.

The day pillar

The one part of the chart with nothing to do with the calendar. The sixty-day cycle has simply been counting, without interruption, for over two thousand years. Any date maps to exactly one day pillar, and we compute it from the Julian Day Number, which is the same continuous count astronomers use.

One convention matters: the day changes at 23:00, not midnight, because 子时 (the Rat hour) begins the new day. A birth at 23:30 on Tuesday carries Wednesday's day pillar.

The hour pillar

The branch is the two-hour 时辰 your birth falls in (the table above). The stem comes from the day stem by a rule called 五鼠遁. A 甲 or 己 day starts its 子 hour with 甲, a 乙 or 庚 day with 丙, and so on.

If you do not know your birth time, you lose the hour pillar and nothing else. Six of the eight characters, including your Day Master, are still fully determined. A three-pillar reading is a real reading.

Why your year may not be the year you think

BaZi runs on the solar calendar, not the lunar one. The twelve months of a BaZi chart are marked by twelve solar terms called 节 (jié), the moments the sun reaches particular points on its yearly path. They are astronomy, not tradition:

The practical consequence. If you were born in January or the first days of February, your BaZi year is the previous calendar year. Someone born on 20 January 2025 is not a Snake, because 立春 had not arrived, so they belong to the Dragon year 2024, and their chart says so.

And a solar term is an instant, not a day. In 2025, 立春 fell at 22:09 on 3 February. Someone born at breakfast that morning belongs to the old year; someone born before midnight belongs to the new one. Both were born on 3 February.

This is why the calculator asks for your birthplace even though you may not want a longitude correction: a term happens at one moment worldwide, so working out which side of it you were born on requires knowing your time zone. Without it, a birth within about fifteen hours of a term genuinely cannot be placed, and the calculator shows you both readings rather than guessing.

True solar time 真太陽時

The hour pillar is meant to describe where the sun actually was, not what a clock on the wall said. Clocks run on time zones, which are a political convenience: everyone in a zone keeps the same time even though the sun reaches them at different moments.

Two corrections turn clock time into sun time:

  • Longitude. The sun crosses your meridian four minutes earlier for every degree you sit east of your zone's centre line. Singapore keeps UTC+8, whose centre line is 120°E, but sits at 103.8°E, so its clocks run about 65 minutes ahead of its sun.
  • The equation of time. Earth's orbit is elliptical and its axis is tilted, so even a perfect sundial runs up to 16 minutes fast or slow depending on the season.

Where this bites hardest. China keeps one time zone across a country five zones wide. A birth in Ürümqi reads more than two hours off its own sun. Anyone born in western China, or in a country with a wide single zone, should expect a real shift, often a whole 时辰.

Daylight saving is the other trap, and no calculator can detect it for you: it is a whole-hour error, exactly half a 时辰, so it changes the hour pillar about half the time. If you were born in summer somewhere that observed it, tick the box. Mainland China observed daylight saving from 1986 to 1991.

Your Day Master 日主

Of the eight characters, one is you: the stem of the day pillar. It is called the Day Master, or 日元, and it is the single most important character in the chart.

Everything else is then read in relation to it. The same character means one thing in a 甲 Wood person's chart and something else entirely in a 庚 Metal person's, because its relationship to the Day Master has changed. This is the idea that makes BaZi different from sun-sign astrology: there are not twelve kinds of people, there is one kind of person per chart and seven other characters describing their world.

How to use it. Read your Day Master's image literally and it will take you further than you expect. 甲 is the tall tree: it grows in one direction, it does not bend easily, it needs room. 乙 is the vine: same element, opposite strategy: it goes around obstacles and climbs on what is already there. Both are Wood. Neither is better.

The five elements 五行

Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. What matters is not the list but the two cycles running through it:

  • Generating (生): Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth (ash), Earth yields Metal, Metal carries Water (condensation), Water grows Wood. Each element is the parent of the next.
  • Controlling (克): Wood breaks up Earth, Earth dams Water, Water puts out Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. Each element restrains the one two places along.

Together they make a star inside a circle, and every relationship in a BaZi chart is one of five: same as me, what I generate, what I control, what controls me, what generates me. Those five relationships are the Ten Gods, below.

The elements panel counts how much of each your chart carries. A chart is not "good" for being balanced. A lopsided chart is simply concentrated, and concentration has its own uses. What matters more is whether your Day Master has support: enough of its own element, and enough of the element that feeds it.

Hidden stems 藏干, and why two counts differ

Every branch conceals one to three stems inside it. 子 hides only 癸. 丑 hides 己, 癸 and 辛. These hidden stems are why a chart with no visible Metal can still behave like a chart with Metal in it.

They are not equal. The first is the main qi (本气) and dominates; a second is the middle qi (中气); a third is the residual qi (余气) and is faint. A common teaching weights them roughly 6 : 3 : 1.

Why the calculator offers two counts. Face value counts each of the eight characters once and ignores what the branches hide. Quick, and the number most calculators show. The Ten Gods panel uses the weighted count, so if you want the two panels to agree, use the weighted one.

The Ten Gods 十神

"Gods" is a translation artefact, and nothing supernatural is meant. A Ten God is just a label for how one character relates to your Day Master. Five relationships, each in two flavours depending on whether the polarities match, gives ten.

Matching polarity (yang meeting yang) is direct and frictionless; opposite polarity (yang meeting yin) attracts and binds. That is why the "direct" and "indirect" labels fall where they do, which looks inconsistent until you see the logic.

The names are old and dramatic. Seven Killings (七杀) sounds alarming and is not: it means pressure that comes at you without a formal relationship softening it: a deadline, a rival, a crisis. People with a lot of it often do their best work under exactly those conditions. Hurting Officer (伤官) means expressive talent that will not sit inside rules; it "hurts" the Officer, which is the rules.

How to read your distribution. Look for what is missing as much as what is abundant. A chart heavy in Output and light in Authority describes someone who generates a great deal and resists structure. Useful to know before you take a job that is mostly structure. None of the ten are good or bad; they describe what you have a lot of and what you will have to build deliberately.

Luck pillars 大运, and the gender question

The natal chart is fixed for life. The luck pillars are what moves: a sequence of ten-year periods, each with a stem and branch of its own, laid over the chart like weather over a landscape. The landscape does not change; what passes across it does.

This is where practitioners spend most of a reading. The eight characters say what you are working with. The luck pillar says what decade you are working in, and the same natal chart reads very differently under a pillar that feeds your Day Master than under one that drains it.

Where they come from

They are not a new calculation. They are simply the sexagenary cycle continued from your month pillar, one step per ten-year period. The month pillar itself is not a luck pillar; the first one is the next step along.

Two things have to be decided: which way to step, and when the first one opens.

Which way: the direction rule

阳男阴女顺行, 阴男阳女逆行. A yang year stem with a male subject, or a yin year stem with a female subject, steps forward through the cycle. The other two combinations step backward.

Yang stems are 甲 丙 戊 庚 壬; yin stems are 乙 丁 己 辛 癸. Only the year stem matters here.

This is the only place in the whole system where the calculator needs to know anything beyond your birth moment, and it is why the form asks. The rule as the classical texts state it is a binary of 男 and 女, and there is no way to derive the direction from a birth moment alone: two people born in the same second get opposite sequences. If you would rather not answer, everything else in the chart is still cast; only this section goes missing.

When: the starting age 起运

Count from your birth to the nearest solar term, the next one if your pillars run forward and the previous one if they run backward, then convert at the classical rate:

Three days stand for one year. So one day is four months, and one hour is five days. Twenty-two days to the next term gives a starting age of about seven years four months.

That rate is why the exact term instant matters so much here. A solar term computed to the nearest day rather than the nearest minute moves the starting age by up to four months, and every subsequent pillar with it. This calculator solves term instants astronomically, so the starting age is exact when your birth time and birthplace are both known, and it tells you the margin when they are not.

Reading them

Each luck pillar is labelled with its Ten God, relative to your Day Master, exactly like the natal characters. That label is the shortest useful summary of the decade:

  • A Resource pillar (印) tends to be a decade of learning, support and consolidation rather than output.
  • A Wealth pillar (财) turns attention outward to results and the material world.
  • An Authority pillar (官杀) brings structure and pressure: responsibility, and the demands that come with it.
  • An Output pillar (食伤) is expressive and generative; things get made.
  • A Peers pillar (比劫) is about independence, competition and the people alongside you.

The honest caveat. A luck pillar is a description of a season, not a forecast of events. It says what kind of pressure and what kind of opportunity the decade tends to carry for a chart like yours. It does not say what will happen, and a "difficult" pillar is not a sentence. For many charts the demanding decades are the productive ones.

Annual pillars 流年

If the luck pillar is the decade, the annual pillar is the year inside it. It is the same sexagenary year the natal year pillar uses, which means you already know how to read it: take its stem, find its relationship to your Day Master, and you have the year's Ten God.

The year turns at 立春, not on 1 January. A birthday in the middle of January belongs to the previous annual pillar. This catches people out constantly, and it is the same boundary that governs the natal year pillar.

Reading a year against a decade

The point of putting them together is that neither one means much alone. Practitioners read the annual pillar against the luck pillar it sits inside, and both against the natal chart. A Wealth year inside a Wealth decade is a very different thing from the same Wealth year arriving in the middle of a Resource decade.

The calculator shows ten years under whichever decade you select. Pick a different ten-year pillar above the row and the years beneath it change to match, so you can walk backward through a decade you remember and see what the framework says about it. That is a more honest test of whether any of this resonates than reading forward into a year that has not happened yet.

What an annual pillar is not

It does not predict events. An annual pillar describes the flavour of a year relative to your chart, in the same descriptive register as everything else here. It says nothing about specific outcomes, and no year in the sequence is a warning. If a particular year's label worries you, that is a sign to close the page rather than to plan around it.

Note also that this calculator stops at the annual pillar. Traditional practice goes further, down to the month and even the day, and layers combinations and clashes between the annual branch and the natal branches on top. Those interactions are exactly what this page does not model, so treat the year row as a starting point rather than a finished reading.

Putting it to use

Read the chart in this order. It is how a practitioner works, and it stops you drowning in detail.

  1. Find yourself. Your Day Master, and its element and polarity. Read its image. That is the protagonist.
  2. Check your footing. Is your Day Master supported? Look at the support figure the calculator gives, which is your own element plus the one that feeds it. Roughly half or more, and you are well rooted; much less, and the chart leans on outside support.
  3. Find the loudest relationship. Whichever of the five families is largest is the theme you keep returning to: output, wealth, authority, resource or peers.
  4. Find the quietest. The smallest family, especially an empty one, is the thing you have to construct on purpose rather than inherit.
  5. Read the pair. The interesting sentence is almost always "a lot of X, very little Y". That is the shape of a life, not a verdict on it.
  6. Then look at the weather. Find the luck pillar you are in now and read its Ten God against what you just worked out. A decade that supplies what your chart is short of feels very different from one that piles on more of what you already have plenty of.
  7. Then narrow to the year. The annual pillar sits inside that decade. Try it on a few years you actually lived through before you read it forward: it is the only way to judge whether the framework tells you anything you did not already know.

A caution worth stating plainly. A chart describes tendencies and the terrain you are working with. It does not predict events, and no arrangement of eight characters obliges you to do anything. Treat a reading the way you would treat a well-observed description of your temperament: useful when it lands, discardable when it does not.

What this calculator does not do

Stated so you know where the edges are, and so you can tell when you need an almanac or a practitioner instead.

  • Branch interactions: the combinations and clashes (六合, 三合, 六冲, 刑, 害) that let branches transform or cancel each other. These can change what an element tally means.
  • A strength verdict (旺衰). We give a transparent support ratio. A real assessment weighs the season heavily, since an element born in its own season is strong almost regardless of how many times it appears, and this calculator does not model that.
  • The useful god (用神): the element your chart most needs. That is the practical output of a professional reading and it depends on everything above.

What it does do, it does exactly: the eight characters, computed from the sun's real position, with every convention stated. That is the part worth getting right, because everything else is built on top of it.
